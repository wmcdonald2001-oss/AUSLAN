    Teach signs

    Give the app short videos of someone signing, one sign per video. It watches each one and learns the sign. The file name is used as the meaning, so name files like hello.mp4, thank-you.mp4 or A.mp4 for a fingerspelled letter. Several videos of the same sign (hello-1.mp4, hello-2.mp4) make it more reliable. You don't need to open the camera for this step.

      Where can I get sign videos?
      • Auslan Signbank has a video for most signs. Its content is licensed CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 (non-commercial, no derivatives). Personal use should be fine, but ask Signbank before sharing sign sets you make from their videos.
      • Research video collections such as MM-WLAuslan (3,215 signs by 73 signers) can be requested for research use. Check their terms before using them.
      • A Deaf friend, teacher or community group could record a few clips on a phone. Clips from real people you'll talk with work best, and a few seconds each is plenty.

      Start the interpreter, type the English word or letter, press Record, then hold the sign for about two seconds. Record each sign 2–3 times, from slightly different angles.
